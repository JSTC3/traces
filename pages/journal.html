01 / Two kinds of footprint

I used to think my digital footprint was my social media: the posts, the profile picture, the comments. That is only half of it. Researchers split it in two. The active footprint is everything I share on purpose: a post, an email, a form I fill in.

The passive footprint is everything collected while I do nothing special: my IP address, the pages I visit, how long I stay, the device I use, the cookies a site stores in my browser. I never typed any of it, yet it forms a surprisingly accurate picture of my habits.

What bothered me most is the imbalance. I can delete a post in one click, but I cannot easily see, let alone delete, what was recorded about me in the background.

// lesson: the footprint I can see is the smaller one.

02 / The photo that knew where I was

Most photos taken with a phone carry hidden text called EXIF metadata. It can include the date and time, the phone model, and, if location is enabled, the GPS coordinates of where the picture was taken. The image looks innocent. The file is talkative.

Many big platforms strip this data when you upload, but not all of them, and not when a photo is sent as a raw file or posted on a small forum. A picture of my desk could, in theory, tell a stranger where my desk is.

I checked my own camera settings and turned off location tagging for the camera app. It took ten seconds. It is the cheapest privacy fix I found.

// lesson: a file can say more than the thing it shows.

03 / The accounts I forgot

I tried to list every account I have ever created. I stopped counting because I kept remembering new ones: a game forum, a free trial, a shop I used once. Each one holds an email address, often a password, sometimes a birth date or a phone number.

Forgotten accounts are a quiet risk. If a small website is breached years later, my old data leaks even though I never use it. And if I reused the same password, one old leak can open newer doors.

A good habit is to check my email address on a breach-notification service such as Have I Been Pwned, then delete the accounts I no longer need. Deleting is boring work, but it shrinks the footprint for good.

// lesson: data does not expire, only my attention does.

29 Oct 2026

04 / Small steps

I will never have a zero footprint, and I do not think that is the goal. Being online has real value. The goal is to choose what I leave behind instead of leaving everything by default.

My short list: use a different password for every account (with a password manager), turn off location tagging on photos, review the privacy settings of the apps I use most, and delete accounts I have not opened in a year. Before posting, I ask one question: would I be fine with this being found in five years?

Traces cannot be erased completely, but they can be managed. That is enough to start.

// lesson: choose your traces, do not just leave them.
